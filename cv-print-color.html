Profile

PhD in Education (University of Glasgow, 2024) on how music teachers in China perceive creativity. Qualitative researcher in music education and creativity, with expertise in interviews, video-recorded observation and thematic analysis. Guest lecturer in qualitative methods at the University of Liverpool. Current work asks whether AI practice tools open, or repeat, women's exclusion from jazz improvisation training.

Research Focus

  • Creativity in music education — core PhD strand: drew on Csikszentmihalyi's Domain-Field-Individual model (the Field as the gatekeeper selecting which novelty is transmitted) to study how examinations, the curriculum and teachers' own training shape their views of creativity, and developed a practice-informed model of teachers' perceptions of creativity, built around a seven-phase Spectrum of Music Education. China's curriculum standards name three areas of creative work: exploring sound, improvisation and composition. Published in Research Studies in Music Education (2026); a second article, on the influence of policy and experience, is in press at Music Education Research.

Independent project

  • Syn-Aesthetica (2025–present). Self-initiated and unfunded, with jewellery-design collaborator Dr Dong Ding, Edinburgh College of Art. Prototype-stage Python/C++ tool designed to combine librosa feature extraction with a self-trained lightweight emotion classifier (public affective-music datasets) to drive physical, kinetic output via Dr Ding's sculptural mechanisms. My role: analysis pipeline, classifier training and the educational-evaluation framework.

Education

01/2019–06/2024 PhD in Education — University of Glasgow

Thesis: Music teachers' perceptions of creativity in the context of twenty-first century Chinese education (viva 27 September 2023; full text). Supervisors: Prof Oscar Odena, Prof Kay Livingston.

Qualitative design: 17 semi-structured interviews, 24 lesson observations, critical-incident charting, researcher diary; thematic analysis in NVivo. Produced a practice-informed model of teachers' perceptions of creativity, including a seven-phase Spectrum of Music Education. Key finding: conservatoire-trained instrumentalists received formal improvisation training; generalist teachers engaged with it only informally.
